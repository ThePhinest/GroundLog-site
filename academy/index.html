The working day

Chapter by chapter

Opening0:24

Observe and report

What the job is, and what GroundLog is for.

Transcript

Ask any environmental inspector what the job is, and you will hear the same three words. Observe and report. See what happened, get it on the record, and be able to prove it. GroundLog is the field notebook built for that job.

This is one working day. Morning to sign off. Everything you are about to see is the app as it ships, on a demo project.

Chapter 20:55

Field map and tracker

Categories, states, layers and the tracker log with the open acreage total.

Transcript

The field map is where GroundLog tracks real work on real ground. Your site, on satellite, with every layer you have drawn.

Every tracker category starts from a template. Seeding, ground disturbance, linear practices like silt fence. Pick the closest one. Everything stays editable.

A category is built from states. Each state has its own color and its own progress. Ground disturbance runs active, inactive, temporary, final, closeout.

Tap any layer and you get its acreage, its date, its state, and what went down on it.

Now open the tracker log. Every state measured against the plan, acre by acre, automatically. And the number the permit cares about. Total open right now, against the five acre cap. Nobody adds it up by hand.

Chapter 30:48

Photos

Stamped photos, tags that feed the reports, pins on the map and links to the work.

Transcript

The Photos page groups your day for you. Newest first, by date. Every one stamped with the time and where you were standing.

Open a photo and tag it. Tag it for the stormwater inspection, or for the seeding record, and it flows to that report on its own.

The stamp is a rendering, not paint. The clean original is always kept, and every element of the stamp can be turned off per photo, forever.

Every photo is also a pin on the map, where you were standing when you took it. Show today's, a date range, or all of them, and tap a pin to see the shot.

From the pin, link the photo to the work it shows. The layer keeps it, and the photo remembers the layer. Evidence, both directions.

Chapter 40:56

Compliance records

Compliance entries, agency visits, spill records and open items.

Transcript

Compliance is a running record, not a form you fill once. Every observation is an entry with a date, a location and a status.

Add one from the field. What you saw, where, how serious, with a photo. It is in the record the moment you save.

An agency walks the site. The visit is its own record. Who came, what they saw, what they asked for. One tap writes the summary into the daily log, and the running visit log exports on its own.

A spill gets the same treatment. What was released, how much, and where. The record asks what the incident form asks, runs the reportability test for your state, and keeps a timed log of who you notified and when.

Open items are your to do list on the site. Add one, give it a due date, and it will find you. Resolve it with a note. The history stays.

Chapter 51:16

Stormwater inspection

A full inspection, from the header to the signature and the export.

Transcript

Stormwater inspections are kept on the Reports page. Here is the running list, newest on top, each one marked draft or completed.

Start today's. The header already knows who you are, your credential and the permit. Everything else carries forward from last time.

The disturbed area summary refreshes from the tracker log. One tap, and the acres are the acres you drew.

Drainage areas and discharge points carry forward from the last inspection. Mark them acceptable in one tap. Flip one to deficient and the note opens.

Every practice from the SWPPP is a row. Installed, condition, maintenance, corrective action.

Corrective actions collect themselves from what you flagged. Add your own if you need to.

Photos you tagged for stormwater since the last inspection are already here.

Sign it on the screen, with a finger or a stylus. Complete locks the record, and offers to carry what you flagged into the compliance log. Reopening asks you to confirm, because this is a regulatory record.

Export a PDF or a Word file in the format the agency expects, and send it from your phone.

How to

One task at a time

Short clips for a single job. More are on the way.

How to1:03

Add seed and amendments to a drawing

Seed, lime and fertilizer on one area, with rates and totals.

Transcript

Seed, lime and fertilizer all go on the record the same way. Start on the map, at the area that was treated, and tap it.

The popup shows what is on file for that area. Open Actions, then Edit.

Scroll down to Applications. One drawing carries as many applications as the work took. The first row is the seed.

Name the mix and enter the rate from the spec. GroundLog multiplies the rate by the acres you drew, and shows what the area requires.

Then enter what actually went down, and how many seed tags you collected.

Add a row for each amendment. Pick lime, name the product, then set the rate and the unit.

Then the fertilizer. Every row writes its own note as you go, so nothing is typed twice.

Save the entry. The tracker log now carries the materials, the rates and the totals, and they flow to the seeding record on their own.

How to1:07

Seed tags and the bag ledger

Tag the photo, attach it to the area, and let the ledger count the pounds.

Transcript

A seed tag is the proof of what went in the ground. Photograph the tag, open the photo, tap the info button, and mark it as a seed tag.

Now attach the tag to the area it was used on. Open the drawing, choose Edit, then Attach Photo. Your tag photos have their own filter. Pick the tag, and tap Done.

Then tap the label under the photo to make it a material tag. Say which product it belongs to, and how many tags are in the shot.

Save the entry.

One thing is left, the bag weight. It is set once per project. In Settings, open Bag weights, name the mix, then enter the pounds per bag and the rate from the spec.

Open the tag photo again, and the ledger has done the math. The tags in the photo, times the bag weight, less what went down on this area. What is left carries to the next one.

The tracker log shows the tags beside the pounds, and the seeding record exports with the tag photos attached.
